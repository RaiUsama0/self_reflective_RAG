Northfield Climate Research Institute

The Northfield Climate Research Institute is an independent research organization
based in Oslo, Norway. It was established in 1994.

The institute employs approximately 260 researchers and operates three regional
field stations: one in Svalbard, one in the Amazon basin, and one in the Australian
outback.

The Svalbard field station has continuously recorded Arctic sea ice thickness data
since 1996, making it one of the longest-running datasets of its kind.

The institute's most recent flagship report, published in 2024, found that average
Arctic sea ice thickness had declined by approximately 65 percent since
measurements began.

The Director of the institute is Dr. Henrik Solberg. The Head of Arctic Research is
Dr. Freya Nystrom.

The institute is funded primarily by the Norwegian government, which provides
approximately 70 percent of its annual budget of 48 million dollars; the remainder
comes from research grants and private donations.

The Amazon field station focuses on deforestation monitoring using satellite
imagery combined with ground sensor networks, covering an area of approximately
50,000 square kilometers.

The institute publishes its Global Climate Bulletin quarterly, and it is freely
available to the public.
