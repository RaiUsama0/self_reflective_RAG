The Harrowgate Museum of Natural History

The Harrowgate Museum of Natural History is located in Edinburgh, Scotland. The
museum first opened to the public in 1887.

The museum houses a permanent collection of over 2.3 million specimens, including
one of the largest fossil collections in the United Kingdom.

The museum's most famous exhibit is a complete Diplodocus skeleton, nicknamed
"Hamish", which has been on display in the Grand Hall since 1962.

Admission to the museum is free for all visitors, though a suggested donation of
5 pounds is requested.

The museum is open daily from 10:00 AM to 5:00 PM, except on 25 December and
1 January, when it is closed.

The current Museum Director is Dr. Fiona MacKenzie. The Head of Paleontology is
Dr. Thomas Reid.

The museum's research department publishes an annual peer-reviewed journal,
the Harrowgate Natural History Review, and currently has 34 active field research
projects around the world.

In 2020, the museum completed a major renovation of its Marine Life wing, adding a
12-meter-long touch pool exhibit.

The museum attracts approximately 900,000 visitors per year, making it the third
most visited museum in Scotland.
